## What he said on the call (2026-09-16)

**Why he stopped the first time.** He subscribed around June/July to do storyboarding for shoot creation. The output hallucinated the colour of the garments in the product images he uploaded — "complete hallucination… the colour of the garment altogether." For an apparel client that is disqualifying: "the customers are quite picky when it comes to product accuracy, and if it goes about hallucinating the colour itself then it's a complete no-go." He stopped using the product. He came back after seeing recent launches and AJ's posts, and re-subscribed on affinity, not on evidence.

**Why the credits are unspent.** Not indifference — deliberate. He was holding his credits until this call so someone could tell him how to use the product for his use case: "I was just saving it for this particular call to happen and then probably understand how can I best use the product." Six months of low usage partly reflects a man waiting for onboarding that never came.

**What he has actually touched.** Monica, chat, Creative Director studio, templates, and "did explore spaces as well." He has not tried the other agents — they didn't fit the job, which is producing content for brands.

**His bar, in his words.** Quality means scroll-stopping, not accurate: "quality is essentially conversion for me." He would accept a hallucinated output that makes people stop and click through, and would reject a 100%-accurate one that is bland — "no one is going to stop, they just doom scroll and go to the next feed."

**The competitive frame.** He has been trying Flora, Higgsfield, Magnificent and the general image models part-time. Higgsfield expands a short prompt well and handles camera animation and B-roll; ShopOS output he described as what he could have got out of Gemini directly. But he puts all of them "in the vicinity of AI slop" — none has creative direction. His framing: Adobe Premiere Pro only a handful can use, then Canva arrived. "When is a Canva moment going to happen in this particular space?" He is not asking for zero-expertise; he is asking to be *probed* — asked questions and have his answers used — instead of being handed a blank box and expected to write a one-page technical prompt with cameras, animations and effects.

**His feedback on the feed prototype**
- He read it as an **inspiration board**, not finished output, and said so within seconds. He flagged the sample shots as generic — "just a model wearing the garment, that's it" — and asked point-blank whether it was a mockup.
- Conditional interest, not enthusiasm: "it lastly depends upon how creative the feed is." If it's generic he'll "scroll and be like this is quite generic, let me just go to chat and start prompting my way to a really good creative."
- He would pay for it — but only on output quality that stands out and converts, not on the mechanic.
- What he liked: not having to prompt, a cadence he could set (he assumed weekly or bi-weekly), and Shobhit's intelligence-layer framing where each generation carries a stated reason ("Diwali is coming, other brands in your category are doing X").
- Credits were his first question about the feed, before any reaction to the concept.
- His verdict on the whole thing: "at the end of the day the buck stops" at the output. The feed changes who initiates the work; it does not answer whether he can trust what comes back.

**What he asked for.** Built-in camera effects and animation templates. Post-production polish — told it only exists on the enterprise services tier (95% AI, 5% human), not self-serve.

## His Monica deep dive (2026-09-17, unsolicited)

Full text at `interviews/sidharth/2026-09-17-sidharth-monica-deep-dive.md`. The most detailed product critique any PLG customer in this base has produced, written unpaid, the day after his first call. Headlines:

- **Storyboards show a different garment than the one uploaded.** His sharpest point, because it is upstream of everything: "seeing a storyboard of a different product immediately reduces confidence in the generation that follows." The approval step actively destroys trust before credits are even spent.
- **Severe product hallucination in final output**, with a reproducible case: chat `7f1361fe-04b8-4f09-b22e-4c19f64ed504`. His framing: for fashion, product accuracy is "a hard requirement, not simply a quality preference" — an aesthetically good video of the wrong garment has no commercial value.
- **The approval loop doesn't build confidence.** Product → direction → storyboard → *credits consumed* → output that doesn't match the approved direction. He wants cheaper previews and pre-generation validation so failure is discovered before the expensive step.
- **Models listed but unavailable** (Kling V3 Omni, Seedance 2.0) — he names this a trust issue, not a bug. Seedance 2.5 and Wan 3.0 missing entirely.
- **Creative Studio overrides brand styling**, and storyboards fall back on the same generic patterns he described verbally: close-up, model turn, basic walk, static shot.
- **Context doesn't persist:** in Video mode he was shown image templates — which he says makes first-time users conclude the platform can't do what they came for, "even when the underlying capability exists."
- Plus: no voiceover preview, ElevenLabs failures without error states, an undismissable right-side pop-up.

His summary of the gap: product fidelity first, creative quality second — and "less like a video generator, more like an actual AI creative studio for fashion brands."
